Studies / Dentists

Dentists in two Ontario cities

We asked AI and Google for the best dentist in Peterborough and Belleville more than 11,500 times.

The short answer

How you type the question changes what you get. In Peterborough, “who is the best dentist” got an AI answer from Google 84% of the time. “Best dentist” got one 1% of the time.

11,500+ searches · 57 dental businesses · 6 platforms · Jul–Aug 2026

What this means for a dental practice

  • The wording decides whether Google shows an AI answer. Type a question and people usually get one. Type a few keywords and they almost never do. Finding 01 ↓
  • Being on Google’s map doesn’t mean being in Google’s AI answer. One practice was in 2 of every 3 AI answers and never on the map. Finding 02 ↓
  • Some practices are never named at all. In Peterborough, 4 of 31 weren’t named once in more than 5,000 answers. Finding 03 ↓
  • Each AI relies on its own few review websites. The site ChatGPT used most wasn’t the one Claude used most. Finding 05 ↓

If you market dental practices, or run a similar local business such as a physio, chiropractic, optometry or vet clinic, the same questions apply to you.

What we found

Seven things we found

No practice is named.

01

Type a full question into Google and you’ll usually get an AI answer. Type a few keywords and you almost never will.

Typed as keywords (“best dentist in Belleville”), Google showed an AI answer only 1–3% of the time. Typed as a question (“who is the best dentist in Belleville?”), it showed one 84–86% of the time. Capital letters, punctuation and “who’s” versus “who is” made no difference. Only whether it was a question.

02

Google’s AI answer and Google’s map often name different dentists.

We took the five practices each one named most. Only three were on both lists in Peterborough, and two in Belleville. One Peterborough practice was in 2 of every 3 of Google’s AI answers, and never once on Google’s map.

Some practices were never named at all.

In Peterborough, 4 of the city’s 31 dental businesses were not named once, on any platform, in more than 5,000 answers.

04

AI named a dentist often only when it had also read the dentist’s own website.

In both cities, no practice was named often unless the AI had also used its own website as a source. We can’t tell which comes first: AI may name the practices whose websites it reads, or read the websites of practices it already names.

05

A few websites supply most of what AI reads about a city’s dentists.

Leave out the practices’ own websites, and five or six review and directory websites supplied most of the sources the AIs used. Each AI had its favourites: ChatGPT relied on one review site, Claude on others, while Perplexity and Google’s AI answer used practices’ own websites most.

06

Some AIs name the dentist. Others name the clinic.

Claude and Gemini named an individual dentist (“Dr. …”) in almost every answer. ChatGPT and Perplexity did in about 60–75% of answers, and more often named the clinic.

07

Ask for “the best” and Claude usually won’t pick one.

Asked “who is the best dentist in [city]?”, Claude said something like “there’s no single best” in more than 9 answers out of 10. Asked for “top dentists”, it almost never did, and just gave a list.

What we asked

Four everyday questions, worded the way people type them:

  • best dentist in [city]
  • top dentists in [city]
  • best family dentist in [city]
  • who is the best dentist in [city]

How we asked

  • Six platforms: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity, Google's AI answer and Google's map results.
  • Over and over: each question repeated many times, across different days and times of day.
  • Free to name anyone: the AIs could name any dentist they liked. We then matched every name against the city's dental businesses: 31 in Peterborough and 26 in Belleville.
  • Every answer kept: recorded in full, with the practices it named and the websites it used as sources.
