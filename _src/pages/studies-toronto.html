Toronto, neighbourhood by neighbourhood

We asked ChatGPT, Gemini and Google's map for dentists in 186 areas of Toronto, more than 14,000 times. Every neighbourhood got its own answer, and neighbours next door can share no dentists at all.

Why it matters

A city-wide number hides what happens street by street.

If AI answered every neighbourhood with the same city-wide list, one measurement would cover the whole city. It doesn't. A practice can be named again and again for its own neighbourhood and not at all two neighbourhoods over. A clinic that tops the answer for "Toronto" can be missing from the answer for the street its patients actually live on.

Most businesses that serve a big city haven't thought about this, and it matters most to the ones with the most at stake:

Groups with several locations

Each location lives or dies in its own neighbourhood's answer, not the city's.

Businesses opening or moving

The neighbourhood you pick decides which answer you're competing to be in.

Agencies and marketers

A client's city-wide number can look fine while their own streets tell a different story.

It isn't only dentists. Any business people look for "near" somewhere, in a city big enough to have neighbourhoods, has the same question to answer.

Along the way

Six things we noticed about how AI handles a city

Patterns from the map. No practice is named.

01

Every neighbourhood gets its own answer.

We expected Toronto's 158 official neighbourhoods to collapse into a few dozen bigger markets. They didn't. Ask about 158 neighbourhoods and, on most platforms, no two of them merge into one market. The only areas that merged were two names for the same place, like "the Annex" and "Annex".

02

Next door is a different market.

Bloor West Village and Etobicoke sit side by side, and the AIs didn't name a single dentist in common for them, on any platform. King West and Liberty Village are a short walk apart and shared almost none. Even downtown, the Fashion District, the Entertainment District and "Downtown Toronto" each got their own list.

03

AI names about the same number of dentists, however many there are.

Across our asks, a neighbourhood with two dentists inside its borders still gets a dozen or so dentists named, drawn from the streets around it. A neighbourhood with fifty gets about the same. The size of the answer barely tracks the size of the market.

~11

dentists ChatGPT named per area across 25 asks, on average, whether the area has 2 or 50+

04

What you call the place changes the answer.

Simple names that mean the same place got the same dentists: "the Junction" and "Junction Area", "Roncey" and "Roncesvalles". But the city's longer official names didn't line up with the name people actually use. "Palmerston-Little Italy" and "Little Italy", or "Kensington-Chinatown" and "Kensington", returned noticeably different lists.

05

Google's map sometimes gives up. The AIs never did.

For three neighbourhoods, Google showed no map results at all, in all 25 tries each. For two more, it mostly showed none. ChatGPT and Gemini named dentists for every one of the 186 areas, every single time.

06

The answers shift over weeks.

We asked about seven of these areas in August and again six weeks later. On ChatGPT and Gemini, roughly half the dentists named had been replaced. Google's map changed less, about a quarter. Within each round the answers were steady; between rounds, they moved.

A note on our numbers

This study used ChatGPT, Gemini and Google's map results; Claude and Perplexity weren't part of it. The final analysis was completed on 26 October 2026. ChatGPT changed its answer format partway through the study; we recorded it and checked that it doesn't change the headline. The six-week comparison can't separate time from model changes, so we report how much the answers moved, not why.
