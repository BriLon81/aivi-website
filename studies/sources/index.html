Why it matters

Being named is the result. The sources are where it comes from.

AI answers are built from other websites: ranking guides, "best of" lists, review sites, directories, forums, news, and businesses' own pages. Two firms can be equally good and still be named very differently, because the pages the AI happens to read list one and not the other. A mention rate tells you where you stand. The sources show you what the AI was reading when it decided.

Along the way

Seven things we noticed about what AI reads

Patterns from dental and legal markets. No business or website is named.

01

Each AI reads from its own shelf.

Same question, same city, different reading lists. In Belleville, one review site was cited 838 times by ChatGPT and not once by Claude. In Toronto law, ChatGPT leaned on a lawyer-ranking guide while Claude leaned on two "best of" pages that the other AIs barely touched.

02

A handful of websites carry most of the weight.

Leaving out the practices' own sites, five or six organisations made up most of what the AIs cited about a town's dentists: 74% in Peterborough and 64% in Belleville.

5–6 sites

behind 64–74% of outside citations

03

Being named goes with being read.

In both dental cities, no practice was named often without the AI also reading its own website. In Toronto law, one well-known firm's website was cited by two platforms and never by the other three. We can't say which comes first: AI may name what it can read, or read what it already names.

04

Much of what AI reads is "best of" lists.

In Toronto law, many of the most-cited pages were "top 10" style articles on general-interest or out-of-town websites, most without a sponsorship label. One was a list of Toronto's top injury lawyers published by a news site in Florida. Peer-reviewed rankings and lawyer directories were in the mix too, but they were far from the whole picture.

05

New pages get read fast.

The single most-cited page in our Toronto law study had been published just ten days before we asked. Four different AIs were already citing it.

10 days

from publication to most-cited page

06

Being cited isn't the same as being named.

One article crowned a law firm "the #1 choice". One AI cited that article in all 50 of its answers, and named that firm in none of them. Reading a page doesn't mean repeating what it says.

50 → 0

answers citing the page vs naming its "#1"

07

Sources say bad things too.

One page that a single AI read in 47 of its 50 Toronto law answers printed complaints about fees and slow responses at four of the city's most-named firms. If an AI is reading it, so is anyone who asks that AI. Most businesses have no idea which of these pages exist.

A note on our numbers

These are patterns we observed, not causes. We record what the AIs cite and what they say; we don't claim that one causes the other. The dental figures come from two cities measured over the summer; the legal figures come from a smaller Toronto study (50 answers per platform), so treat those as early signals.
