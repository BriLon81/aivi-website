Studies / Ontario downtowns

Where does AI point people in an Ontario town?

Downtown associations exist to bring people downtown. Some of the people they're trying to reach now ask AI where to go first. So a downtown study answers a simple question: when people ask AI where to eat, shop or spend a day in town, how much of the answer points downtown? These are the first five Ontario towns we've measured.

The short answer

In all five towns, AI named downtown places about two to three and a half times as often as downtown's share of places.

Downtown holds between 9% and 32% of each town's restaurants, cafés, bars, shops and attractions. Between 34% and 64% of the places AI named were downtown.

Why it matters

Downtowns count who walks in. This counts what AI tells people before they choose.

The answer decides which part of town someone hears about first. It also changes whenever the AIs change, which is why a downtown study is repeated each quarter.

What we found

Three findings about downtowns

01

Downtown gets more than its share in every town.

In each town, we put every place the AIs named on a map and checked whether it sits inside the downtown business improvement area (BIA). We then compared that with downtown's share of the town's places on Google.

A place counts each time it's named. Downtown is the area of each town's BIA: the Downtown Barrie BIA, the Cobourg Downtown BIA, the Downtown Guelph Business Association, the Downtown Kingston BIA and the Downtown Stratford BIA.

02

Every platform agrees.

On ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity and Google's AI answer, in every town, the share of places named downtown was above downtown's share of places. The lowest single result was 26%, in a town where downtown holds 9% of places.

Each platform in each town rests on 40 asks, so single results are indicative. Even the low end of each range sits above downtown's share.

25 of 25

platform-and-town results above downtown's share of places

03

Dining out leads. Things to do trails.

Across the five towns, 63% of the restaurants, cafés and bars named were downtown, against downtown's 20% share. For shops it was 59% against 15%. For things to do (attractions, museums, parks, theatres) it was 35% against 12%, the smallest lead of the three.

What this shows about AI

Five things we learned about the AIs themselves

Asking the same questions in five towns also shows how each platform behaves.

A

Google answers food questions with a map, and weekend plans with AI.

For the five searches about restaurants, coffee, brunch and the best shops, Google showed its map results every time (125 of 125) and an AI answer only 14 times. For "things to do", "what to do this weekend" and "where to go shopping", it never showed the map, and it gave an AI answer 72 times out of 75. The wording matters as much as the topic: "where to go shopping" got an AI answer in 25 of 25 searches, while "best shops" got the map instead.

B

Ask a different AI, get a different-length list.

Claude named about 11 places per answer and Gemini about 10. Perplexity named about 8 and ChatGPT about 4. ChatGPT also named no place at all in 9 of its 200 answers. Every other platform named at least one place in every answer it gave.

C

AI cites the town's tourism website.

In three of the five towns, the website the AIs cited most was the town's tourism site: visitkingston.ca in 113 of 200 asks, experiencecobourg.ca in 104, and tourismbarrie.com in 86. In Stratford, visitstratford.ca came second (49), after tripadvisor.ca (65). In Guelph, the most-cited site was a local news site, guelphtoday.com (55). The downtown association's own website was among the ten sites cited most in three of the five towns.

D

AI treats events as places.

Asked where to go, the AIs also named 355 events, tours and trails, such as festivals and walking routes. We left those out of the downtown counts, because they don't have a single address.

E

Google's map results lean downtown too.

Where Google showed its map, the share of places that were downtown was higher than on the AI platforms in all five towns. This one is indicative: the map shows about two places per search.

What we asked

Eight everyday questions, asked the same way in every town:

  • best restaurants in [town], Ontario
  • where should I eat in [town], Ontario
  • best coffee shop in [town], Ontario
  • best place for brunch in [town], Ontario
  • where to go shopping in [town], Ontario
  • best shops in [town], Ontario
  • things to do in [town], Ontario
  • what to do in [town], Ontario this weekend

These are first-look wordings. A full study sets its questions from evidence of how people in that town actually ask, pilots several wordings, and publishes them.

How we asked

  • Six platforms: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity, Google's AI answer and Google's map results.
  • The free versions: the models each platform gave free users at the time (ChatGPT gpt-6-luna, Claude Sonnet 5.5, Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite, Perplexity Sonar).
  • From the town: ChatGPT, Claude and Perplexity were told the asker was in the town. Gemini was asked without a location; the town is in every question. Google searches were run from each town.
  • Five times each: every question asked five times on every platform, over two sittings: the evening of November 8 and the morning of November 9, 2026 (Eastern time).
  • Every place mapped: each place named was matched to its Google listing, or found with a Google Maps search, then tested against the BIA's boundary. Barrie, Cobourg and Kingston publish their BIA boundaries as open data. For Guelph and Stratford we traced the boundary from the BIA's own published map.
  • Checked by hand: we matched 93% of the places named to a location (6,672 of 7,184). A person checked 50 matches by hand: 49 were right, 1 was unclear, and none was wrong.
  • Downtown's share of places: every restaurant, café, bar, shop and attraction Google lists within 5–8 km of downtown, depending on the town's size, and the share of them inside the BIA.

A note on our numbers

These are patterns we observed on November 8–9, 2026, not causes, and AI answers change. This is a first look: two sittings, 200 AI asks per town, 40 per platform. That's lighter than the standard we use for paid studies, so single-platform results are indicative. A place counts each time it's named. Places we couldn't locate (7%) are left out of the shares. Even counting every one of them as outside downtown, the downtown share stays above downtown's share of places in every town (Barrie 31%, Cobourg 57%, Guelph 43%, Kingston 57%, Stratford 61%). Google's AI answer counts only when Google showed one with text (86 of 200 searches; one more showed an empty box). The AI figures in the first three findings exclude Google's map results, which are reported separately.
