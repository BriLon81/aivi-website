Studies / Ontario downtowns

Where does AI point people in an Ontario city?

When people ask AI where to eat, shop or spend a day in a city, how much of the answer points downtown? We asked in five Ontario cities: Barrie, Cobourg, Guelph, Kingston and Stratford.

The short answer

In all five cities, AI named downtown places far more often than downtown's size would suggest. In Kingston, only about 1 in 5 of the city's places are downtown. But about 3 in 5 of the places AI named were downtown.

1,200 asks · 5 cities · 6 platforms · November 8–9, 2026 · a first look, from two sittings

What this means for a BIA

  • AI points people downtown far more than downtown’s size would suggest. It happened in all five cities, on every AI we tested. Finding 01 ↓
  • It leans downtown most for restaurants and shops. For things to do, the gap was smallest. Finding 03 ↓
  • The wording decides what Google shows. Searches for restaurants, coffee, brunch and “best shops” got Google’s map. Searches for things to do and weekend plans got an AI answer. Finding A ↓
  • The website AI used most was usually the city’s own tourism site. The downtown association’s own website was in the top ten in three of the five cities. Finding C ↓

If you run a downtown association, a main street program or a business district anywhere, the same questions apply to you. They also matter to the businesses inside it, to businesses elsewhere in the city that AI names less often, and to the city’s tourism and economic development offices.

What we found

Three findings about downtowns

01

In every city, AI named downtown far more than downtown's size would suggest.

We put every place the AIs named on a map and checked whether it was inside the city's official downtown area (its business improvement area, or BIA). Then we compared that with how many of the city's restaurants, cafés, bars, shops and attractions are downtown.

A place counts each time it's named. Downtown is the area of each city's BIA: the Downtown Barrie BIA, the Cobourg Downtown BIA, the Downtown Guelph Business Association, the Downtown Kingston BIA and the Downtown Stratford BIA.

02

Every AI we tested did it, in every city.

We checked each AI (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity and Google's AI answer) in each city separately. Every one named downtown more than its share, in every city. Even the lowest result, 26% of places named downtown, came from a city where only 9% of places are downtown.

Each AI in each city is based on 40 questions, so single results are rough. But even at the low end of their likely range, every one is above downtown's share.

03

AI leans downtown most for restaurants and shops, least for things to do.

Across the five cities, downtown has 20% of the restaurants, cafés and bars, but 63% of the ones AI named were downtown. Downtown has 15% of the shops, but 59% of the shops named. For things to do, like museums, parks and theatres, it was 12% and 35%, the smallest gap of the three.

What this shows about AI

Five things we learned about the AIs themselves

Asking the same questions in five cities also shows how each platform behaves.

A

Google answers food questions with a map, and weekend plans with AI.

When we searched Google for restaurants, coffee, brunch or "best shops", it showed its map every time (all 125 searches) and an AI answer only 14 times. When we searched for "things to do", "what to do this weekend" or "where to go shopping", it never showed the map, and gave an AI answer 72 times out of 75. Small wording changes matter: "where to go shopping" got an AI answer all 25 times, while "best shops" got the map.

B

Ask a different AI, get a different-length list.

Claude named about 11 places per answer and Gemini about 10. Perplexity named about 8 and ChatGPT about 4. ChatGPT also named no place at all in 9 of its 200 answers. Every other platform named at least one place in every answer it gave.

C

The website AI used most was usually the city's own tourism site.

In three of the five cities, the website the AIs used most as a source was the city's tourism site: visitkingston.ca (in 113 of 200 asks), experiencecobourg.ca (104) and tourismbarrie.com (86). In Stratford it was the Stratford Festival's site, stratfordfestival.ca (72), ahead of tripadvisor.ca (65) and the city's tourism site, visitstratford.ca (49). In Guelph it was a local news site, guelphtoday.com (55). The downtown association's own website was among the ten sites used most in three of the five cities.

D

AI suggests events and trails, not just places.

Asked where to go, the AIs also named 355 events, tours and trails, such as festivals and walking routes. We left those out of the downtown counts, because they don't have a single address.

E

Google's map results lean downtown too.

When Google showed its map instead, an even bigger share of the places it listed were downtown, in all five cities. Treat this one as rough: the map lists only about three places per search.

What we asked

Eight everyday questions, asked the same way in every city:

  • best restaurants in [city], Ontario
  • where should I eat in [city], Ontario
  • best coffee shop in [city], Ontario
  • best place for brunch in [city], Ontario
  • where to go shopping in [city], Ontario
  • best shops in [city], Ontario
  • things to do in [city], Ontario
  • what to do in [city], Ontario this weekend

These are first-look wordings. A full study sets its questions from evidence of how people in that city actually ask, pilots several wordings, and publishes them.

How we asked

  • Six platforms: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity, Google's AI answer and Google's map results.
  • The free versions: the models each platform gave free users at the time (ChatGPT gpt-6-luna, Claude Sonnet 5.5, Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite, Perplexity Sonar).
  • From the city: ChatGPT, Claude and Perplexity were told the asker was in the city. Gemini was asked without a location; the city is in every question. Google searches were run from each city.
  • Five times each: every question asked five times on every platform, in two sittings on November 8, 2026: the afternoon and the evening (Eastern time). Some of the second sitting's Google searches ran the next morning, November 9.
  • Every place mapped: each place named was matched to its Google listing, or found with a Google Maps search, then tested against the BIA's boundary. Barrie, Cobourg and Kingston publish their BIA boundaries as open data. For Guelph and Stratford we traced the boundary from the BIA's own published map.
  • Checked by hand: we matched 93% of the places named to a location (6,672 of 7,184). A person checked 50 matches by hand: 49 were right, 1 was unclear, and none was wrong.
  • Downtown's share of places: every restaurant, café, bar, shop and attraction Google lists within 5–8 km of downtown, depending on the city's size, and the share of them inside the BIA.

A note on our numbers

These are patterns we observed on November 8–9, 2026, not causes, and AI answers change. This is a first look: two sittings, 200 AI asks per city, 40 per platform. That's lighter than the standard we use for paid studies, so single-platform results are indicative. A place counts each time it's named. Places we couldn't locate (7%) are left out of the shares. Even counting every one of them as outside downtown, the downtown share stays above downtown's share of places in every city (Barrie 31%, Cobourg 57%, Guelph 43%, Kingston 57%, Stratford 61%). Google's AI answer counts only when Google showed one with text (86 of 200 searches; one more showed an empty box). The AI figures in the first three findings exclude Google's map results, which are reported separately.
