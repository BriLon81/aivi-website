Ask twice: how consistent are AI answers?

Ask an AI the same question twice and the businesses it names often change. We asked identical questions thousands of times, on ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Perplexity and Google. Any single answer changes a lot, but over many asks a business's rate can be measured to within a few points. It's like a coin: one flip tells you little, a hundred flips tell you the odds.

Why it matters

One answer is an anecdote.

If someone shows you a screenshot of ChatGPT recommending your business, or leaving you out, they've shown you one roll of the dice. Ask again and the list can change. Ask next month and the AI itself may have changed. Decisions made on one answer are decisions made on luck.

The useful question isn't "were we named?" It's "how often are we named, and has that really changed?"

Along the way

Six things we noticed about how AI answers move

Patterns from repeated questions across our studies. No business is named.

01

Over many asks, the rates are steady.

Measured properly, a business's rate on ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Perplexity is accurate to within 2–4 points. That's what makes a rate trustworthy, and a single answer not.

±2–4 pts

margin on a measured rate, current models

02

But any single list changes a lot.

Ask Gemini the same question twice and, of all the businesses it names across the two answers, only about 35–40% appear in both. Claude is steadier at around 55–60%. Google's map is the steadiest, at around 80%.

03

Who gets named first depends on the platform.

On Google's map, the same business comes first in about 8 of every 10 repeats. On Gemini, the first name changes far more often, and a single question can produce half a dozen different "first" answers over a study.

04

An update nobody announced moved businesses 30 points.

In July, Google quietly switched the version of Gemini most people use. Same questions, same town, old version against new: one practice went from being named 54% of the time to 84%. Another fell from 75% to 46%. The normal wobble is a couple of points.

54→84%

one practice, after the update

75→46%

another, same update

05

AI answers can change shape overnight.

On 8 October, in the middle of one of our studies, ChatGPT's answers changed format from one day to the next: longer, bolder, more like a list. Earlier version changes on the same questions halved answer lengths, or made an AI far more likely to say "it depends".

219 → 361

median words per ChatGPT answer, 7 vs 8 October

06

Some AIs drift day to day. Others vary on every ask.

Perplexity's answers on the same day look more alike than its answers on different days, so it drifts over time. Gemini varies just as much within an hour as across days. Its wobble is in every single answer, not in the calendar. That's why we spread every study across several days and times.
