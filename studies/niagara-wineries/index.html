Studies / Niagara-on-the-Lake wineries

Where does AI send people wine tasting in Niagara-on-the-Lake?

We asked ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity and Google the questions visitors ask about wine tasting in Niagara-on-the-Lake, 2,304 times, and recorded every winery they named.

The short answer

Five of Niagara-on-the-Lake’s 40 wineries get more than 40% of the mentions. And about 1 in 7 answers sends people to a winery that has closed, usually one still listed on an out-of-date web page the AI had read.

2,304 asks · 40 wineries · 6 platforms · November 13–15, 2026

What this means for a winery

  • Five wineries get more than 40% of the mentions. The other 35 wineries share the rest. Finding 01 ↓
  • Two companies own 1 in 8 of the wineries but get about 1 in 3 of the mentions. Ask for small, family-run wineries, and it drops to about 1 in 20. Finding 02 ↓
  • About 1 in 7 answers names a winery that has closed. Finding 03 ↓
  • When an AI reads an old page, it often repeats it as if it were current. Nearly every answer that named a closed winery had read a page that still lists it. Finding 04 ↓

What we found

Five things we found

01

Five wineries get more than 40% of the mentions.

Asked about Niagara-on-the-Lake, the AIs mentioned 38 of its 40 wineries. Five of them got more than 40% of the mentions: Peller Estates, Two Sisters, Ravine, Inniskillin and Trius. Peller Estates and Two Sisters made every platform’s top five. Ask about plain “Niagara”, and they get about half.

02

Two companies own 1 in 8 of the wineries but get about 1 in 3 of the mentions.

Arterra (Inniskillin, Jackson-Triggs) and the Peller group (Peller Estates, Trius, Wayne Gretzky Estates) own 1 in 8 of the wineries, but got about 1 in 3 of all the mentions. The AIs do follow the question, though: asked for small, family-run wineries, the two companies got only about 1 in 20.

03

About 1 in 7 answers names a winery that has closed.

About 1 in 7 chat answers named a winery that has closed. The ones named most were Small Talk Vineyards, which closed in 2022; Frogpond Farm, which closed its winery this year; and The Ice House, which Google lists as permanently closed. How often it happened depends heavily on the platform: Claude did it in about 1 in 4 answers, ChatGPT almost never.

04

When an AI reads an old page, it often repeats it as if it were current.

Nearly every answer that named a closed winery had read a web page that still lists it, and all but one named the winery as a place to go, with no warning that it had closed. The biggest source is a MapQuest article on small Niagara wineries. As of November 18, 2026, it still lists Small Talk and Frogpond, and calls Bella Terra by its old name, Pondview.

Even asked directly whether The Ice House is open, Gemini and Perplexity said yes, 10 times out of 10. ChatGPT and Google’s AI Overview got it right.

05

One word changes the answer.

Ask about “Niagara”, and about 6 in 10 chat answers named wineries outside Niagara-on-the-Lake, in places like Beamsville and Jordan. Say “Niagara-on-the-Lake”, and fewer than 1 in 10 did.

Also found

What this shows about the AIs

Each AI reads different sources.

ChatGPT cited a winery’s own website in almost every answer. Perplexity rarely did, and leaned on review and booking sites instead. The site cited most often overall was Destination Ontario’s.

Winery’s own site: ChatGPT 367 of 384 answers, Perplexity 36 of 384. Review and booking sites: Perplexity 336 of 384. Destination Ontario: 690 answers.

Google writes an AI answer for questions, not keywords.

Asked as a full question, Google showed an AI Overview almost every time. Typed as keywords, it did about 1 time in 4.

Full questions: 185 of 192 searches. Keywords: 49 of 192.

Google’s map results and the AIs disagree.

Between the Lines Winery was the winery Google’s map results listed most for Niagara-on-the-Lake, in about 6 of every 10 searches that showed them. It isn’t in the AIs’ top five, and the AIs put it first only when asked for small, family-run wineries.

Listed in 101 of the 168 searches that showed map results.

Tour companies are almost never named.

Across all 16 questions, the AIs named a wine tour company in about 1 in 50 chat answers, almost all of them on the ice wine question.

33 of 1,536 chat answers.

What we asked

Four things visitors want, each asked two ways: as typed into a search box, and as a full question. We chose them from evidence of how visitors search and ask, and tested the wordings in a pilot first.

  • best wineries niagara on the lake
  • My partner and I are driving down from Toronto for a day of wine tasting in Niagara-on-the-Lake. Which three or four wineries should we visit?
  • winery restaurant niagara on the lake
  • We’re spending a Saturday wine tasting in Niagara-on-the-Lake and want a nice lunch at a winery. Where should we book?
  • ice wine tasting niagara on the lake
  • We’re visiting Niagara-on-the-Lake this winter and want to try ice wine. Which wineries should we go to?
  • small wineries niagara on the lake
  • We’ve been to the big wineries in Niagara-on-the-Lake before. Which smaller, family-run ones are worth visiting this time?

We asked all eight again with “Niagara” in place of “Niagara-on-the-Lake” (“best wineries in niagara”, and so on): 16 questions in all.

How we asked

  • Six platforms: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity, Google’s AI Overview and Google’s map results (the short list of places Google shows above the web links).
  • The free versions: the models each platform gave free users at the time (ChatGPT gpt-6-luna, Claude Sonnet 5.5, Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite, Perplexity Sonar).
  • From Toronto: ChatGPT, Claude and Perplexity were told the asker was in Toronto, and the Google searches were run from Toronto. Gemini can’t be given a location, so it was asked without one.
  • 24 times each: every question was asked 24 times on every platform, spread over eight sittings across three days, November 13–15, 2026.
  • Every winery counted: we matched each name against a list of the 40 Niagara-on-the-Lake wineries with their own tasting room, built from each winery’s website before the main run. Closed wineries, old names and wineries outside the town were counted separately.
  • Checked by hand: a person checked 50 answers. A computer cross-check found that wineries written in short form (such as “The Hare Wine”) had been missed. We fixed the lists and recounted; the top-five, two-company and closed-winery figures moved by less than half a point.
  • Old pages read: on November 18, 2026, we tried to read every page cited by an answer that named a closed winery, and could read 140 of the 206; the rest couldn’t be read.

A note on our numbers

These are what the AIs said on November 13–15, 2026, and AI answers change. Each question was asked 24 times per platform. That’s lighter than the standard we use for paid studies, so the headline shares are accurate to within about 4 to 8 points either way, and single-platform figures are a direction, not a precise number. A winery counts once per answer, however many times the answer names it. A closed winery counts whenever an answer names it (one of the 221 answers named it only to say it had closed), but not when it appears only as a former name of another winery. We read the cited web pages on November 18, 2026, not when the AIs read them, so finding 04 shows a strong link, not proof that a given page caused a given answer. Google’s AI Overview counts only when Google showed one.
